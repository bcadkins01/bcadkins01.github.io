About me

An uncommon overlap: clinic, bench, and code.

The path here wasn't a straight line. Enzyme engineering and assay design on an industry bench, then pharmacy school, a community counter, and over ten years of clinical toxicology, first at a poison center and then on a consult desk. Fifteen thousand consultations, give or take. A detour through web development in 2018 that taught me I liked the data more than the interface, and a master's in data science in 2024. Most of what I make now sits somewhere in that overlap.

H:L (hit to lead)

Allegheny Biosciences · drug discovery platform

The drug discovery platform I run at Allegheny Biosciences. It puts a connected set of open source drug discovery tools under one roof, so designing molecules does not start with setting up environments and cloning repos. What someone points it at is up to them. Antibiotic resistance and pandemic preparedness are what I'm interested in, and the beta-lactam families came first for that reason.

Drug-Likeness Classifier

My data science master's capstone, built from scratch. An XGBoost model that predicts drug-likeness from a SMILES string, using RDKit Morgan fingerprints and Lipinski descriptors as features. Paste a structure and it returns the prediction, the computed properties, and a rendered molecule. Small model, but the whole path from a chemical input string to a served prediction is in there.

Beta-Lactam Generator

A "can I actually do this" experiment, where the question was less about chemistry. Train a generative model on beta-lactam scaffolds, then get it deployed and hosted somewhere anyone could use it. Early runs generated molecules closer to gasoline and jet fuel. What is working now is beta-lactams, albeit mediocre ones. That is fine. Getting the whole path working was the win, and output quality is the part that practice fixes. Better beta-lactam models now live on H:L, free to use.
